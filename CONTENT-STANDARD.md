# Content Standard

*What a topic must contain before it counts as written. This file replaces the
"Content contract" boilerplate that was generated into 123 topic READMEs — that block described
work rather than doing it, and it made an empty folder read like progress.*

**Last revised: 2026-08-10**

---

## The rule

> **A topic is written when someone who has never seen the subject can read it once and then
> explain the mechanism to an interviewer, including at least one number, one command, and one
> failure they could hit.**

Prose that could have been written without understanding the product does not qualify. The test
is specificity: if a sentence would survive being copied into a different product's
documentation, it is filler.

---

## Required sections

Every topic `README.md` carries these, in this order. Skip a section only when it genuinely does
not apply, and say why.

| # | Section | Must contain |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What it is** | Two or three sentences. No hedging, no "in today's world". |
| 2 | **Why it exists** | The concrete problem that existed before it. What people did instead, and what that cost. |
| 3 | **How it works underneath** | The mechanism. Data structures, protocols, order of operations. **A diagram.** |
| 4 | **Worked example** | ⭐ **Real values.** An actual attribute, payload, token, query, or config — traced end to end. This is the section that was missing everywhere. |
| 5 | **Commands** | Runnable, with the **shape of the expected output**. A command with no expected output cannot be verified by the reader. |
| 6 | **When and where** | Decision criteria. When to choose it, and what to choose instead. |
| 7 | **What breaks** | ⭐ Real failure modes with **the actual error text**, and the diagnosis path. |
| 8 | **Customer discovery questions** | What to ask in a real engagement to find out how this is configured. |
| 9 | **Remember it** | ⭐ **A mnemonic or hook, an analogy that rebuilds the mechanism from scratch, and the one line that regenerates the whole topic.** Explaining without giving retention aids is lecturing, not teaching. |
| 10 | **Self-test** | 5–8 questions answerable only if the reader understood the mechanism. Answers included. |
| 11 | **Evidence this topic needs** | Which of the six facet folders, and what artifact goes in each. |

### On section 9

Every hook added here must also be folded into [`RETENTION.md`](RETENTION.md), which is the
**interleaved** memory layer across all topics. Interleaving beats blocked practice for durable
recall — that is why the deck lives in one file rather than as 22 separate appendices, and why the
per-topic block stays short.

A good analogy is **load-bearing**: the reader who has forgotten the detail can rebuild it from the
analogy alone. "Kerberos is like a theme park wristband" earns its place because it predicts that
the ride operator never phones the gate — which is exactly why Silver Tickets leave no DC log.
A decorative comparison that predicts nothing does not qualify.

## Numbers, not adjectives

Replace every vague quantity with the measured one.

| ✗ Filler | ✓ Standard |
|---|---|
| "syncs frequently" | "every **2 minutes**, not configurable" |
| "uses strong hashing" | "**PBKDF2**, **1,000 iterations**, **HMAC-SHA256**, **10-byte** per-user salt" |
| "large groups may have issues" | "the `groups` claim overflows at roughly **150–200** groups and is replaced by `_claim_names`" |
| "tokens are short-lived" | "access token default **60–90 minutes**, variable; refresh token **90 days** inactive" |

If the number is not known, write **`⚠ unverified`** next to it rather than inventing precision.

## Verification marking

Carried over from the DAY files, and it applies everywhere:

- **`✅ verified`** — run, or read from a live Microsoft doc, and confirmed. Cite the date.
- **`⚠ check`** — correct to the best of current knowledge, **not** re-verified today.
- **`✗ unrunnable`** — blocked in this tenant. Say what licence or resource it needs.

An unmarked claim is treated as `⚠ check`.

---

## What disqualifies a topic

1. **Boilerplate that describes intent.** "Explain, implement, secure, operate…" is a plan.
2. **No worked example.** Concept prose alone is section 1–3 of 10.
3. **Commands without expected output.** The reader cannot tell success from failure.
4. **Links that 404.** CI enforces this (`.github/workflows/coverage.yml`).
5. **Claiming hands-on where none happened.** Evidence folders hold artifacts, not intentions.
