# The practitioner traps — where five years of experience makes you answer wrong

**Built 2026-09-14. Exam Friday 18 September 2026, 07:30 IST.**

> **Read this last, on Thursday, and again in the car.** Everything else in this pack teaches
> you material. This file is about a different failure mode: the places where you already know
> the right answer *operationally* and the exam wants a different one.

> **Why this is the highest-value file here.** A candidate with two years' experience reads a
> stem, recalls the documented behaviour, and answers it. You read the same stem, recall what
> you would actually deploy on Monday morning, and answer *that*. On perhaps a dozen items
> across the paper those are different answers, and the difference is worth more than any
> single domain you could revise.

**The one-line version:** *the exam asks what the product does, not what you would build.*

---

### 1. Global Administrator is the reflex and it is always wrong

Five years of operating means he has held GA and used it for everything, so the role that comes to mind first is the one that can do the task. The exam's stem word 'least privilege' converts that reflex into a guaranteed loss. Worse, the correct answers are often roles he has never assigned in his life: Groups Administrator when the task is only group management (not User Administrator, which is broader), Cloud Application Administrator when the task is enterprise apps but explicitly excludes Application Proxy (Application Administrator is the one that can manage App Proxy — that single capability is the entire difference), Identity Governance Administrator for access packages and reviews rather than a privileged role.

### 2. Authentication Administrator versus Privileged Authentication Administrator

In production he resets MFA for whoever raises the ticket and never thinks about the boundary. The exam tests it precisely: Authentication Administrator can reset passwords and re-register authentication methods for non-administrators and for holders of a few low-privilege roles only; Privileged Authentication Administrator can do it for ALL users including Global Administrators. If the scenario says 'the helpdesk must be able to reset MFA for any user including administrators', the answer is Privileged Authentication Administrator; if it says 'helpdesk, least privilege, standard users', it is Authentication Administrator and picking the privileged one is the trap.

### 3. Answering Conditional Access questions with what he would deploy instead of what the engine…

Operationally he thinks in rollout terms: exclude break-glass, start report-only, ring the pilot group. The exam asks what happens when three overlapping policies evaluate. The mechanics he must answer from, not his deployment habit: ALL policies matching the sign-in are evaluated; a Block grant in any one of them wins over every Grant; exclusions always beat inclusions regardless of which policy they are in; within one policy multiple grant controls can be combined with Require all or Require one, but ACROSS policies every policy's grant requirement must be satisfied simultaneously.

### 4. 'Require multifactor authentication' when the question says phishing-resistant

He has built dozens of CA policies with the Require multifactor authentication grant control and it is still the default motion. Since authentication strengths shipped, a stem containing 'phishing-resistant', 'FIDO2 only', 'certificate-based only' or 'must not allow SMS' wants a built-in or custom authentication strength selected in the grant control. Require MFA accepts any registered method including SMS and is therefore the distractor.

### 5. Per-user MFA, the legacy MFA service settings page, trusted IPs and app passwords

This is five years of muscle memory pointing at a retired surface. Microsoft retired the ability to manage authentication methods in the legacy MFA and SSPR policies on 30 September 2025 (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/authentication/concept-authentication-methods-manage). The exam's expected answers are the converged Authentication methods policy for which methods are available and to whom, Conditional Access for when MFA is required, and named locations in place of trusted IPs. The complication he must hold in his head: the material sometimes still shows the legacy surface, so recognise it, but never choose it as the way to configure something new.

### 6. MSOnline and AzureAD cmdlets

Set-MsolUser, Connect-MsolService, Get-AzureADUser, New-AzureADGroup are typed from reflex after five years and every one of them is retired — deprecated March 2024, MSOnline retirement executed April–May 2025, AzureAD unsupported after 30 March 2025 (https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoft-entra-blog/action-required-msonline-and-azuread-powershell-retirement---2025-info-and-resou/4364991). In a four-option PowerShell question, the Msol- and AzureAD- options are there specifically to catch experienced administrators. The answer is the Mg cmdlet, or Microsoft Entra PowerShell.

### 7. Entra Connect Sync as the default answer because it is what his employers ran

The exam has shifted the default the other way. Cloud Sync is the answer when the scenario has multiple disconnected forests, an acquisition or merger with no forest trust, a need for high availability through multiple lightweight agents, or the words 'no additional on-premises server'. Connect Sync is the answer only when the scenario needs something Cloud Sync cannot do: device writeback and Microsoft Entra hybrid join, Exchange hybrid writeback, pass-through authentication, federation, or complex custom filtering and transformation rules. Picking Connect Sync because it is the grown-up product is a habit, not an analysis.

### 8. Choosing federation because his environment had AD FS

The blueprint bullet is literally 'Migrate from AD FS to other authentication and authorization mechanisms' — the exam's direction of travel is away from federation. Password hash synchronisation is the answer to 'minimum on-premises footprint', 'highest availability', 'fewest servers', and 'lowest cost'. The trap with teeth: Microsoft Entra ID Protection's leaked credentials detection requires password hash synchronisation. A PTA-only or federated tenant gets nothing from it, so a scenario that requires leaked-credential detection forces PHS even where the customer prefers PTA, and staged rollout is the mechanism for moving there without cutting the federation over.

### 9. Off-boarding by disabling the account

Every practitioner's muscle memory for a departure is: block sign-in, remove licences, convert the mailbox. The exam's D2 bullet is 'Disable accounts and revoke user sessions' as two distinct actions because disabling the account does not invalidate tokens already issued — an existing refresh token can keep working for up to its lifetime. The complete answer is disable AND revoke sessions (Revoke-MgUserSignInSession, or Revoke sessions in the portal), with Continuous Access Evaluation shortening the window to near-real-time for CAE-capable clients and services. If the stem says 'immediately' or 'the user must lose access to Exchange Online right now', disable alone is wrong.

### 10. Conflating the three ways an outsider gets in, because the admin centre puts them side by side

B2B collaboration creates a guest user object in the directory. B2B direct connect creates NO object at all and exists only for Teams shared channels — so 'the partner's users must not appear as objects in our directory' forces direct connect. Cross-tenant synchronisation automatically provisions users from a partner tenant as B2B objects in yours, which is the multi-tenant-organisation and merger answer. And cross-tenant access settings (inbound and outbound trust, whether you accept the partner's MFA and compliant-device claims) are a different blade from External collaboration settings (who may invite, domain allow and deny lists). Practitioners routinely say 'guest settings' for all four.

### 11. Granting admin consent because that is what a Global Administrator does

His reflex when a user hits a consent prompt is to grant tenant-wide admin consent and move on. The exam wants the admin consent workflow — users submit a request, designated reviewers approve, the request is auditable — whenever the scenario mentions 'users should be able to request', 'the security team must review', or 'without granting Global Administrator to the approvers'. And it wants user consent settings restricted to verified publishers for selected low-impact permissions, not consent turned off entirely, when the constraint is 'allow productivity apps but stop risky ones'.

### 12. System-assigned managed identity as the automatic answer

It is the right default in production and the exam knows it, so it asks the distinguishing cases. A user-assigned managed identity is required when the identity must outlive the resource, be shared across several resources, or be granted permissions BEFORE the resource exists (pre-provisioning for a deployment pipeline). A system-assigned identity is deleted with its resource, which is the whole point of the question. And when the workload does not run in Azure at all — a GitHub Actions pipeline, an on-premises script, a Kubernetes cluster elsewhere — the answer is workload identity federation, not a managed identity and not a client secret.

### 13. Reaching for Application Proxy for everything on-premises

That was the only answer for years and it stopped being the only answer. With Global Secure Access now a full D2 subsection, the split is: Application Proxy for HTTP and HTTPS web applications with pre-authentication; Microsoft Entra Private Access for anything non-HTTP — RDP, SSH, SMB file shares, any TCP or UDP port — and for replacing a VPN. A stem naming a port, a legacy client-server application, or 'the application has no web interface' is a Private Access question, and App Proxy is the trap answer precisely because it is the experienced administrator's first thought.

### 14. Thinking of Defender for Cloud Apps as 'the CASB we bought' rather than four separable…

Cloud Discovery consumes firewall and proxy logs (snapshot upload, continuous log collector, or native Defender for Endpoint integration) and produces a shadow-IT report — it discovers, it does not block. Conditional Access App Control requires a Conditional Access policy whose session control routes the session through the MDA reverse proxy, and is what makes inline session policies possible. Session policies block download, copy, paste, print in real time. App-enforced restrictions is the one that is NOT MDA at all — it is enforced by SharePoint Online and Exchange Online themselves (limited web-only access, no download), needs no reverse proxy and no MDA licence. A practitioner says 'block downloads on unmanaged devices' and reaches for MDA; if the stem says 'without additional licences' or names only SharePoint and Exchange, the answer is app-enforced restrictions.

### 15. Break-glass accounts configured from folklore rather than from the current guidance

The exam expects: cloud-only, never synchronised from on-premises; a permanent ACTIVE Global Administrator assignment, not a PIM-eligible one (the point is that they work when PIM or MFA is broken); excluded from Conditional Access policies that could lock them out; using a strong authentication method that does not depend on a specific phone or person; at least two of them, stored separately; and monitored with an alert on sign-in. The trap most often set is 'make the break-glass account eligible in PIM' — it looks like good hygiene and defeats the entire purpose.

### 16. Trusting report-only mode and the What If tool the way he trusts a production change window

Report-only logs what WOULD have happened and does not apply the controls, so it cannot prove a session control works. What If is a simulation against conditions you type in — it does not evaluate real risk state, real device compliance, or real sign-in context. When the question is 'a user was blocked, how do you determine which policy did it', the answer is the Conditional Access tab of that user's entry in the sign-in logs, not What If. Practitioners reach for What If because it is the tool they open when planning.

### 17. Dynamic groups as the universal answer, and the role-assignable group rule

Habit says dynamic group for everything, but a role-assignable group must be created with the role-assignable flag set AT CREATION (it cannot be converted afterwards), must be assigned membership and NOT dynamic, must be a security group or Microsoft 365 group, and can only be managed by Privileged Role Administrator or Global Administrator. A question that says 'assign the group to the Helpdesk Administrator role AND populate it automatically from department' is unanswerable as stated, and the exam expects him to notice. Related: mail-enabled security groups cannot be used for group-based licensing or role assignment; dynamic user and dynamic device rules cannot be mixed in one group.

### 18. PIM answers drawn from his own tenant's configuration rather than from what the product does

After five years of activating roles daily, his intuition reports his employer's settings — eight-hour activations, approval required, justification mandatory — as if they were product behaviour. They are per-role settings, configurable per role, and the exam tests the mechanism: eligible requires activation and active does not; assignments can be permanent or time-bound; Entra role settings and Azure resource role settings are two separate surfaces in PIM; PIM for Groups makes membership OR ownership of a group itself eligible, which is the answer whenever a scenario needs just-in-time access to something that is not an Entra role, such as an access package or an Azure resource granted through a group.

### 19. Access reviews judged by what his organisation did with them rather than by the settings

The exam's favourite item is the behaviour when a reviewer does not respond: the 'If reviewers don't respond' setting offers No change, Remove access, Approve access, or Take recommendations, and the default is No change. Second favourite: a review of a group's membership removes the user from the group, and nothing else — it does not remove a directly assigned licence or a directly assigned role. Third: a guest access review can be set to block sign-in for 30 days and then delete the account, which is the answer to 'remove dormant external users automatically'.

### 20. Answering the Yes/No problem-solution sets with 'that is not how I would do it'

This is the most expensive habit of all, because the sets cannot be revisited and never appear on the review screen. The criterion is strictly 'does this solution meet the stated goal', not 'is this the best practice', not 'is this how a good engineer would build it'. Multiple solutions in the same set can independently be Yes. An experienced practitioner rejects a technically correct but inelegant solution on aesthetic grounds and loses marks that a less experienced candidate takes.

### 21. Product names

He says Azure AD, AAD Connect, Azure AD Premium P2, MCAS, Azure AD Identity Protection. The exam says Microsoft Entra ID, Microsoft Entra Connect Sync, Microsoft Entra ID P2, Microsoft Defender for Cloud Apps, Microsoft Entra ID Protection, Microsoft Entra Internet Access, Microsoft Entra Private Access. This costs nothing when the names map one to one, and costs a mark when an answer option uses an old name for a thing that no longer exists or has been split — Microsoft Entra Permissions Management and Microsoft Entra Verified ID being the two most commonly mis-mapped.

### 22. 'I would script it.' Five years of engineering means the PowerShell answer genuinely is…

Group-based licensing over a licensing script. A dynamic membership rule over a scheduled sync job. A lifecycle workflow over a Logic App. An access package over a ticketing process. An access review over a quarterly spreadsheet. The built-in feature wins every time that phrase appears, even when he knows the script would be better.

## What NOT to study — things that feel productive and score nothing

- Touching the app, the markdown files or the lexicon. Adding questions, fixing a heading, regenerating the HTML, writing another discriminator — all of it feels like studying and scores nothing. Content creation is his known displacement activity, and the pack is already past the point where marginal content adds marks. As of Monday, the pack is frozen. The only permitted write is the miss-log from the practice assessment.
- The Applied Skills assessment lab APL-0501 'Get started with identities and access using Microsoft Entra'. It is genuinely free, genuinely a real tenant, and genuinely the wrong use of two hours this week. Its published task list is: create a user, add a licence, invite an external user, assign a role, bulk import, create groups, view password protection lockout settings, view SSPR properties and methods, enable or disable per-user MFA, view trusted IPs, review a Conditional Access policy, run What If, enable or disable a policy (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/applied-skills/resources/study-guides/apl-0501). A five-year Entra engineer can do every one of those in his sleep. It tests nothing he is weak at. Take it next week as a free credential, not this week as revision.
- Writing KQL. The blueprint bullet is 'Monitor Microsoft Entra ID by using KQL queries in Log Analytics' and the audience profile says to be familiar with KQL, but SC-300 tests whether you can pick the right table and read a query, not author one. Learn four table names and stop: SigninLogs, AADNonInteractiveUserSignInLogs, AuditLogs, AADProvisioningLogs. Do not spend an hour on joins, summarize, bin, or render.
- Microsoft Entra Connect Sync internals. Synchronization Rules Editor, the metaverse, connector space, inbound versus outbound rule precedence, declarative provisioning expressions. Real hybrid work lives here and it is seductive because he knows it. SC-300 asks Connect Sync versus Cloud Sync as a selection decision, plus staging mode, plus filtering at a conceptual level. Rule authoring is not on the paper.
- Protocol internals. SAML assertion structure, OIDC discovery documents, PKCE, token signing certificate rollover procedures, decoding a JWT by hand. Read exactly four claims and stop: aud, iss, scp, roles. The scp-versus-roles split is already covered in EXPLAIN/D3-WORKLOAD-IDENTITIES.md §2 and GAP-DRILL.md §6 — that is the depth the exam wants.
- Memorising all the built-in Entra roles. There are dozens; roughly twelve decide answers. The ones that matter are in GAP-DRILL.md §2 already: Global Administrator, Privileged Role Administrator, Privileged Authentication Administrator, Authentication Administrator, User Administrator, Groups Administrator, Application Administrator, Cloud Application Administrator, Conditional Access Administrator, Security Administrator, Security Reader, Global Reader, Identity Governance Administrator. Learning the other forty is pure cost.
- Azure RBAC authoring. Custom role JSON, Actions, NotActions, DataActions, assignable scopes. SC-300 touches Azure resource roles only through PIM for Azure resources. The deep RBAC material belongs to AZ-104 and AZ-500.
- Microsoft Graph REST endpoints and request bodies. Knowing that a user is created by POST to /v1.0/users with a JSON body earns nothing. Recognising New-MgUser in an answer option earns a mark.
- Dump sites. Three days out, under money pressure, ExamTopics and its equivalents will look like a shortcut. They are stale against an exam that changed on 27 April 2026, their community answers are frequently wrong, and using them violates the Microsoft Certification Exam Candidate Agreement he must accept on screen Friday morning. A revoked certification is a worse outcome than a failed one.
- Watching a video course end to end. John Savill's SC-300 cram and Microsoft's own four-part Exam Readiness Zone series are good material — in week one. In the last three days, passive intake displaces active recall and produces the feeling of preparation without the retrieval practice that actually moves a score. If he wants one, cap it at the Global Secure Access and Defender for Cloud Apps segments only, at 1.5x, Wednesday, as a coverage check against BLIND-SPOTS.md.
- Re-reading the four EXPLAIN files cover to cover. He wrote them, he has read them, and re-reading produces recognition ('yes, I know this') which is a well-documented illusion of competence. Use the 'Say it back — cover the right column' sections instead. Same files, retrieval instead of review.

## The verdict, quoted rather than paraphrased

> The pack is sufficient to pass, and probably with margin, but that judgement is currently unfalsifiable — which is itself the finding. Coverage against the verified 27 April 2026 blueprint is genuinely broad: I grepped the whole of C:\IT\azure-cloud-security-mastery\SC-300-SPRINT against the study guide's bullets and found real content behind almost all of them, with the three formerly-missing groups (Global Secure Access, Defender for Cloud Apps, monitoring and logs) now taught in BLIND-SPOTS.md, 83 confusion pairs in DISCRIMINATORS.md, and logistics in FINAL-FOUR.md that are more thorough than anything Microsoft or Pearson publishes in one place. The genuine holes are small and cheap: device join and registration and the tenant device settings blade (about 40 minutes), the tenant and user settings toggles (25), Entra Kerberos and cloud Kerberos trust (20), external identity providers and self-service sign-up user flows (25), and the D3 tail of application collections, the cloud app catalog and OAuth app policies (20) — roughly two and a half hours of Tuesday, all of it targeted at named bullets he currently cannot answer. But every one of the 180 questions, 126 flashcards, 83 pairs and 274 lexicon terms was authored inside this project, so the pack cannot detect its own errors and he has no external score anywhere. The single highest-value remaining action, today, before any of the content work: sit the official free practice assessment (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exams/sc-300/practice/assessment?assessment-type=practice&assessmentId=60) cold, on a 100-minute clock, with no notes and no Microsoft Learn, and log every miss against the blueprint bullet it maps to. That is the only instrument he has that measures his knowledge against Microsoft's wording rather than his own, and it converts three and a half days of guessing at what to revise into three days of revising what the data says is weak. Second priority, also today and not Thursday: put the passport physically next to the Microsoft Learn certification profile and compare the first and last name character by character, because the Pearson VUE exception window closes around tomorrow and that failure mode ends the sitting before a question is asked.
