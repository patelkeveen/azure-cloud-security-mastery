# 75 — Architecture and Consulting

> ⭐ **What this is:** the layer that turns *"I did labs"* into *"I have delivered engagements."*
> ⭐ **It is what an interview panel is probing when they ask a scenario question**, and it is
> almost entirely absent from certification material.

**When to read it:** ⭐ **before your next interview**, and before any customer-facing work.
⭐ **Read it even if you have no customers** — the discipline applies to internal projects, and the
vocabulary is what makes you sound senior.

**Prerequisites:** none technically. ⭐ It lands harder after you have built something.

---

## Why this domain exists

⭐ **Most failed engagements were technically successful.** The design was correct and the wrong
person approved it, or the operator who inherited it could not run it, or there was no evidence the
requirement had been met.

⭐ **The governing rule across every topic here: decide it in daylight.** Abort criteria, approver
independence, acceptance tests, handover gates — ⭐ **all of them are cheap to agree in advance and
impossible to agree under pressure.**

---

**Build and change:**

| # | Topic | ⭐ The one thing it gives you |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | [`configuration-checklists`](configuration-checklists/) | ⭐ ≤9 items at a named pause point; ⭐ every item says **how it was verified** |
| 6 | [`cutover-playbooks`](cutover-playbooks/) | ⭐ **Lead never types; Verifier ≠ Executor**; ⭐ dated abort criteria |
| 7 | [`change-management`](change-management/) | ⚠ **adoption**, not change control; ⭐ ADKAR as a *diagnosis* |

**Hand it over:**

| # | Topic | ⭐ The one thing it gives you |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | [`sop-and-runbooks`](sop-and-runbooks/) | ⭐ written for **03:00, second week**; ⭐ `Last tested`, not `Last updated` |
| 9 | [`customer-training`](customer-training/) | ⭐ **sit on your hands and watch them do it** |
| 10 | ⭐ [`handover`](handover/) | ⭐ **never hold a credential you haven't watched them change** |

⭐ **Topic 1 and topic 10 are the same conversation nine weeks apart.** ⭐ **"Handover refused" is
always a discovery failure surfacing at the end** — the service desk lead who first sees the system
at handover has every reason to reject it.
